In this task you design a **serial communication interface** to talk to a touchscreen and obtain the coordinates of the point that has been touched.

## Goals

The goal of this lab is to learn the characteristics and use of a touchscreen, which is the application the design focuses on. To do so you will develop and implement a serial communication interface of the kind usually used with analogue-to-digital converters (ADCs). You will go deeper into the Verilog HDL concepts used to design control elements. The **functional verification methodology** is very important in this lab and relies on behavioural models of a serial interface. Finally, you will continue to practise physical verification of the design on hardware.

## LTM touchscreen

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/pantalla-tactil-ltm/imagen042.png]]

This lab uses the LTM touchscreen made by Terasic for the Altera University Program boards such as the DE2-115. It is a 4.3-inch screen with a 15:9 aspect ratio and a resolution of 800 × 480 pixels at 24 bpp, able to show about 16 million colours. It has an analogue-to-digital converter that captures the touch point.

### Structure of the LTM touchscreen

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/estructura-pantalla-tactil-ltm/imagen043.png]]

In this screen, display and touch capture are completely independent, so touch capture can be used on its own without displaying anything, as in this lab. Touch capture is handled by an analogue-to-digital converter (ADC).

### Touch characteristics

The Analog Devices **AD7843** is a **12-bit** ADC that provides the X and Y coordinates of the touch point. These coordinates are read through its serial interface.

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/caracteristicas-_touch_/imagen044.png]]

Both the screen's serial interface and the ADC's are connected to the FPGA through the 40-pin connector. Because of the limited number of pins, they share some signals: the same clock (**ADC_DCLK**) and the same enable (**SCEN**). To prevent the two communications from interfering, the ADC's enable is inverted: when the LCD is enabled (SCEN low) the ADC is disabled, and vice versa.

Besides these two shared lines, the ADC's serial interface has:

- **ADC_DIN**: data input, used to send control commands to the ADC.
- **ADC_DOUT**: data output, through which the ADC sends the 12 bits of the X or Y coordinate serially.
- **ADC_BUSY**: indicates that the ADC is busy.
- **ADC_PENIRQ_n**: interrupt request indicating that the screen has been touched.

### Timing diagram

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/diagrama-de-temporizacion/imagen047.png]]

The figure shows the timing diagram of the communication protocol with the ADC to obtain the X or Y coordinate. The **FPGA is the master** of the communication and the ADC is the slave. The FPGA therefore generates the select signal **SCEN**, the **ADC_DIN** signal, used to send the coordinate request command, and **ADC_DCLK**, which sets the communication speed and synchronises the bits on ADC_DIN and ADC_DOUT. All other signals are generated by the ADC.

Although the FPGA controls the communication, it must know when to start it: when something touches the screen, which the ADC signals through **ADC_PENIRQ_n**.

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/diagrama-de-temporizacion/imagen053.png]]

ADC_PENIRQ_n is normally high and goes low when the screen is touched with a pen or a finger. The FPGA must monitor it: when it goes low, it must start the communication protocol with the ADC to obtain the coordinates.

The protocol starts by activating **SCEN** and generating **ADC_DCLK** and **ADC_DIN** as shown in the timing diagram. A control command requesting the X or the Y coordinate is sent through ADC_DIN; the table gives the command for each one. Since they are different, **two communications** are needed, one per coordinate. When the ADC receives the command, it performs the conversion and sends the 12 bits of the coordinate serially on ADC_DOUT, synchronised with ADC_DCLK.

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/diagrama-de-temporizacion/imagen055.png]]

ADC_DIN must stay low when it is not being used to send the command. Also, despite its name, **ADC_DCLK is not really a clock signal**: it is low when idle and only toggles during the communication, setting the speed and synchronising the bits. One transmit/receive sequence for a coordinate lasts **24 ADC_DCLK periods**, with a maximum period of 0.1 ms and a minimum of 0.5 µs, i.e. a speed between **10 kbps and 2 Mbps**.

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/diagrama-de-temporizacion/imagen054.png]]

The lab consists of designing a serial communication interface. Here it is applied to a touchscreen: the design must control the ADC that captures where a pointer or a finger has been placed.

## Sub-task 1: Serial interface to the ADC

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/subtarea-1-interface-serie-del-convertidor-adc/imagen045.png]]

Design the serial communication interface with the ADC to obtain the X and Y coordinates of the touch point. The design must follow the AD7843 timing shown above. The block has a clock input for the 50 MHz oscillator, an active-low reset, the communication signals to the ADC and two 12-bit output buses with the X and Y coordinates.

> [!note] Two different speeds
> The design runs on a **50 MHz** clock, while the communication must run at between **10 kbps and 2 Mbps**.

### Suggestion: half-cycle counter

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/sugerencia-contador-semiciclos/imagen048.png]]

You can use a counter that keeps track of the **half-periods** needed to complete the transmit/receive sequence: **48 counts** in total, corresponding to the half-periods of the 24 ADC_DCLK periods. The count changes are used to generate ADC_DCLK and ADC_DIN and to capture the bits received on ADC_DOUT.

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/sugerencia-contador-semiciclos/imagen056.png]]

Pay special attention to when the bits are placed on **ADC_DIN**: they must change when the counter changes, coinciding with the **falling edge** of ADC_DCLK. For example, bit A0 is placed on the change from count 5 to 6 and lasts for counts 6 and 7. The half-period counter is therefore a good reference for generating both ADC_DCLK and ADC_DIN.

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/sugerencia-contador-semiciclos/imagen057.png]]

The bits sent by the ADC on **ADC_DOUT** must be captured in the **middle** of each bit, where the line is most stable: for example, bit 9 on the change from count 22 to 23 and bit 8 between counts 24 and 25. The capture coincides with the **rising edge** of ADC_DCLK, although the reference is the counter value.

### Suggestion: concatenating the sequences

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/sugerencia-concatenacion/imagen058.png]]

Obtaining both coordinates requires two consecutive transmit/receive sequences. However, each sequence contains waiting times, which can be removed by **concatenating** the two sequences.

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/sugerencia-concatenacion/imagen059.png]]

In the diagram, the request for the Y coordinate starts while the X coordinate is still being received. This is possible because the transmit and receive lines are independent, and it takes advantage of the ADC's waiting time. The complete sequence for both coordinates therefore lasts only **80 half-periods**.

<iframe src="../../static/animaciones/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/sugerencia-concatenacion/Temporizacion_Tactil01.html" width="720" height="400" style="border:0;max-width:100%"></iframe>

### Proposed design structure

Based on concatenating the two sequences, the following block diagram can be used as a starting point. All blocks use the 50 MHz clock and the reset signal.

<iframe src="../../static/animaciones/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/propuesta-estructura-diseno/diagrama_bloques_bi/Diagrama_bloques_bi.html" width="720" height="400" style="border:0;max-width:100%"></iframe>

- On the left, a counter with modulus **ADC_DCLK_CNT** (a parameter) that sets the transmission speed between 10 kbps and 2 Mbps. Besides the terminal count (TC), it generates a signal that goes active at **half count** (TC/2).
- In the centre, the **modulo-80** counter, the reference for the 80 half-periods of the complete sequence.
- Below, in yellow, the **ADC_DCLK generator**, which uses the terminal count of the first counter.
- On the right, the **ADC_DIN generator**, which depends on the half-period count, and the **ADC_DOUT capture** block, which uses the half-period count and, as enable, the half-count signal of the first counter.
- At the top, a **state machine** that controls the whole design by generating the necessary enable signals. It starts the sequence from **ADC_PENIRQ_n** and generates the select signal **SCEN**.

The **ADC_BUSY** line is not used in this proposal.

### Proposed state machine

The state machine that manages the protocol is not unique and can be designed in many ways. This is one proposal; both diagrams represent the same machine in different notations (ASM chart and state diagram).

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/propuesta-fsm/imagen060.png]]

It is a four-state machine:

- **S0**: initial state after reset. It is transient and is used to initialise the signals.
- **S1**: waits for the screen to be touched. It stays here while ADC_PENIRQ_n is 1 and moves to S2 when it becomes 0.
- **S2**: starts the protocol by activating the enables (of the counters) and the ADC chip select. It stays here until the X and Y coordinates have been obtained, i.e. until the 80 ADC_DCLK half-periods counted by the modulo-80 counter are complete.
- **S3**: transition state that ends the protocol and signals its completion with **Fin_Trans**.

### Design verification

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/verificacion-del-diseno/imagen061.png]]

Once the interface has been described in Verilog HDL, free of compilation errors, it must be functionally verified, i.e. simulated to check that it works correctly.

Simulating the communication with the ADC by generating the signals by hand would be complicated. Instead, a **functional model of the touchscreen** that emulates the ADC communication is used: **EMULE_ADC.vo**. Verification is done in ModelSim with a verification environment written in Verilog HDL.

To make this easier, the Quartus project [[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/subtarea-2-visualizacion-del-punto-de-contacto-de-la-pantalla/touch_alumno.qar|Touch_alumno.qar]] has been prepared, fully configured and with all the necessary files. It includes **ADC_CONTROL.v**, the interface you must design, which is empty except for the module ports.

#### Testbench

The verification environment in Touch_alumno.qar has the structure shown in the image. The testbench **tb_Touch_Control.v** is a structural description that connects the block under verification, **ADC_CONTROL.v**, to the screen emulator, **EMULE_ADC.vo**. Both are wrapped by **MFB.v**.

<iframe src="../../static/animaciones/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/verificacion-del-diseno/diagrama_verificacion_bi/Diagrama_verificacion_bi.html" width="720" height="400" style="border:0;max-width:100%"></iframe>

MFB generates the clock and reset. It then simulates a touch: it gives X and Y coordinates to the emulator and activates **NEW_COORD** to start the emulation. The emulator drives ADC_PENIRQ_n low, to which ADC_CONTROL must respond by starting the protocol; the emulator then sends it the coordinates provided by MFB.

When ADC_CONTROL has obtained the coordinates, MFB compares them with those given to the emulator and prints in the ModelSim console whether the verification passed or failed.

#### Procedure

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/procedimiento/imagen035.png]]

1. Download and unpack the Touch_alumno.qar project.
2. Design the interface in **ADC_CONTROL.v**, which already contains the module ports. This module must always be the **top level** of the hierarchy, because it is the design synthesised into the FPGA. The files tb_Touch_Control.v, MFB.v and EMULE_ADC.vo are only used for simulation: they are not part of the design and are not synthesisable.
3. Compile the design without errors.
4. Launch the simulation with **Tools > Run Simulation Tool > RTL Simulation**. The project is already configured, so nothing needs adjusting.

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/procedimiento/imagen036.png]]

#### Checking your design

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/comprobacion/imagen033.png]]

When the simulation is launched, ModelSim opens. The testbench hierarchy is on the left and the waveforms of the ADC interface on the right.

The simulation runs automatically. If the design works, the console shows the message "**Verificación del DUV correcta**" (DUV verification passed).

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/comprobacion/imagen034.png]]

Otherwise, one of these may happen:

1. **The simulation does not start**, because ModelSim has found an error in ADC_CONTROL that Quartus let through. Go back to Quartus, fix it and relaunch the simulation.
2. **The simulation starts but never ends**, because ADC_CONTROL does not work properly: usually the protocol never finishes because its signals do not return to their idle values.
3. **The simulation ends with "Error de Transmisión"** (transmission error), because the received coordinates do not match those sent.

Once ADC_CONTROL passes this initial simulation, extend the verification with other cases. Modify **tb_Touch_Control.v** to add a second case with **three consecutive communications of three different values, without activating reset between them**. Add it from line 137, where it says "CASO 2", using the "CASO 1" lines as a reference but **without** the `MFB.reset` function.

When this verification passes, move on to the physical verification of ADC_CONTROL.

### Physical verification

Once the board is configured, the first test people usually do is touch the screen and check that the displays show the coordinates. Whether it works or not, the proper approach is to check the serial communication by observing the signals with a **logic analyser**. Quartus includes one, **SignalTap**, which is embedded in the design and lets you see the FPGA's internal signals.

### Checking and upgrading the design

Once the serial communication has been checked, finish the verification by checking the values on the displays, bearing in mind that:

**The corners are hard to reach.** It is practically impossible to see coordinates 12'h000 or 12'hFFF on the display, because the touch surface is very small compared with the touch resolution and because of the limits imposed by the methacrylate frame. Do not worry if you cannot reach the manufacturer's theoretical values.

**The last reading when you lift your finger may be wrong.** With the finger resting on the screen the coordinates are stable and roughly correct, and dragging it changes them according to the movement. But when you stop touching, the last value changes to a coordinate unrelated to the last position (sometimes only one of them changes). This happens because while the screen is touched the ADC reads continuously, and when you release it, it still performs a last reading once the finger has gone, so the resistance at the point changes. It can be solved by adjusting two parameters:

1. **The communication speed.** It can be between 10 kbps and 2 Mbps; in the proposed design it is set by the **ADC_DCLK_CNT** parameter, initially at about 70 kbps. Changing it can minimise the effect, although it may not be enough and you may need to combine it with the next adjustment.
2. **A pause between two consecutive acquisitions**, so that the ADC does not convert continuously. When the screen is touched the coordinates are acquired, and no new communication takes place until at least **half a second** has passed, even if the finger is still on the screen. This means fewer readings and avoids the wrong ones. The drawback is that the design must be slightly modified to add an element that generates this wait, of half a second or an adjustable value.

<iframe src="../../static/animaciones/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/verificacion-y-mejora-del-diseno/diagrama_bloques_mejora_bi/Diagrama_bloques_mejora_bi.html" width="720" height="400" style="border:0;max-width:100%"></iframe>

To reduce the number of readings, the proposal is to add a **modulo-25,000,000** counter, which with the 50 MHz clock introduces a half-second delay between one reading and the next. It is at the bottom left of the new block diagram.

This counter requires changes to the ADC_CONTROL state machine. The best option is to add a wait state **S4** after the communication has finished and the coordinates have been obtained. In S4 the machine enables the counter and stays there until it reaches its terminal count, so no new communication takes place until the half second has elapsed.

The block diagram shows the extra inputs and outputs the machine needs, and the figure shows the state diagram with the new state.

![[assets/diseno-de-una-comunicacion-serie/verificacion-y-mejora-del-diseno/imagen065.png]]

Afterwards, adjust the counter modulus to obtain the most suitable delay for the transmission speed.
